🎯 The "Simulation-First" Paradigm

Why We Never Build the Physical Arm Before Simulation

Beginners often think: "Should I build the robot first, then teach it?" Modern robotics does the exact opposite:

  • 1. Zero Hardware Breakage: An untrained AI moves completely at random in its first 50,000 steps. In real life, erratic movements strip plastic gears, snap belts, and burn motor coils. In MuJoCo physics, a crash resets in 1 millisecond at $0 cost.
  • 2. Hyper-Speed Cloud Parallelism: Mastering 3D reaching requires ~300,000 trial-and-error attempts. On a physical arm at 1 step/sec, that takes 83 hours of motor wear. On a Google Colab GPU with 16 parallel virtual arms, it finishes in 15–20 minutes.
  • 3. Instant Mechanical Tuning: If an arm link is 2cm too short to reach a table corner, we edit one number in the XML file in 10 seconds—avoiding 12+ hours of CAD remodeling and 3D printing.

📚 Key Terminologies Demystified

Homography Mapping

💡 Analogy: Taking an angled photo of a rectangular notebook—the photo shows a tilted trapezoid.

A mathematical formula (3x3 matrix) that un-skews camera perspective, converting 2D camera pixels (u, v) directly into real-world table coordinates (X, Y) in meters.

Synthetic Test Harness

💡 Analogy: A flight simulator for pilots or crash-test dummy rig.

A test script feeding mock/simulated camera frames into our AI code to verify object detection and JSON output without needing real cameras or live hardware plugged in.

Domain Randomization (DR)

💡 Analogy: Practicing tennis on grass, clay, and in high wind so you can win on any court.

Randomly perturbing mass, friction, and latency during simulation training so the AI policy transfers seamlessly to physical 3D-printed hardware (Sim-to-Real).

ONNX Runtime

💡 Analogy: Exporting a finished Hollywood film to a compact MP4 video.

A standardized compact neural network format. Trains on heavy cloud GPUs (PyTorch), then runs on local PC CPUs in <5ms without heavy AI dependencies.
