Mastering Cloud PPO Training, Domain Randomization & ONNX

A hands-on guide for beginners to teach a virtual 6-DOF arm to move in 3D space.

🎯 Grounded in the Mission: Before we assemble physical 3D-printed links or motors, we train our AI policy in Google DeepMind MuJoCo on Google Colab. A virtual crash costs $0 and resets in 1 millisecond.

1. The Four Core Pillars of Ticket 02

To train our 6-DOF desktop robot arm to reach any 3D coordinate on a table, we rely on four interconnected concepts:

A. Vectorized Parallel Environments (Hyper-Speed Training)

In standard robotics simulation, running a single simulated arm takes 83 hours to complete 300,000 trial-and-error movements. By using Vectorized Environments (SubprocVecEnv), we spawn 16 parallel virtual robot arms simultaneously on Google Colab.

Instead of 1 step at a time, we collect 16 experience steps simultaneously, boosting throughput to over 2,000 frames per second (FPS). Your AI completes 300,000 learning steps in just 15 to 20 minutes on a free cloud GPU.

B. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)

PPO is an Actor-Critic algorithm that balances exploratory learning with stability:

  • The Actor (The Motor Driver): A neural network taking the 27-dimensional observation vector (joint angles, velocities, end-effector position, and target position) and outputting 6 continuous joint velocity/angle deltas.
  • The Critic (The Evaluator): A neural network that estimates the future cumulative reward (distance to goal) from the current state.
  • The PPO Clip: During updates, PPO penalizes drastic policy changes (clipping the objective between $1-\epsilon$ and $1+\epsilon$, typically $\epsilon = 0.2$). This prevents the neural network from taking catastrophic leaps and unlearning prior skills.

C. Domain Randomization (Bridging the Reality Gap)

In pure MuJoCo simulation, physics are mathematically pristine: friction is uniform and link masses are exact. On a real 3D-printed desk arm:

  • PLA plastic bearings have varied friction ($10\%-20\%$ variance).
  • Micro-servos have command transmission latencies ($10\text{ ms}-30\text{ ms}$).
  • Print infill causes slight variations in link mass ($\pm 15\%$).

Domain Randomization (DR) deliberately perturbs these physical values across episodes during training. By practicing in a thousand varied virtual worlds, the AI policy develops robustness and transfers smoothly to real hardware (Sim-to-Real).

D. ONNX (Open Neural Network Exchange)

PyTorch is great for training on cloud GPUs, but requires gigabytes of dependencies to run locally. ONNX serializes the trained actor network into a lightweight, platform-agnostic computational graph.

Your local host PC runs the policy using onnxruntime in under 5 milliseconds per forward pass on a standard CPU, with zero PyTorch or CUDA dependencies.
